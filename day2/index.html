Day 2 — Group Testing and Interview

Ground tasks with your group, then the Deputy President's interview for about half the group. Everything below is drilled the way the board runs it: a topic you did not choose, a clock you cannot stop, and a group that has to finish together.

1 Group Discussion

Assesses communication skills, reasoning, and the ability to work collaboratively on a given topic. No chairman, no voting — the group has to reach a common line by itself.

15:00
  • Open only if you have a point worth making — the first speaker is remembered for what he said, not for being first.
  • Give a reason and an example with every opinion.
  • Bring in the man who has not spoken; that counts more than speaking twice.
  • Never shout down a member. Accept a better point openly.
  • Watch the clock and push the group towards a conclusion near the end.

2 Progressive Group Task (PGT)

A series of physical obstacles that the group must navigate together, testing teamwork and leadership. Four obstacles of rising difficulty, with a load, a plank, a rope and rules you must not break.

  • The three standing rules: the group, the load and the helping material all cross — nothing and nobody is left behind.
  • Out-of-bounds areas, colour-banned zones and the rule about the plank never touching the ground are what get groups sent back.
  • Suggest a plan in one sentence, show it with your hands, then get on the obstacle.
  • If your plan is dropped, work the accepted plan at full effort.
  • Help the weakest man across first; the group is timed as a group.

3 Half Group Task (HGT)

Similar to the PGT but with smaller groups, focusing more on individual contributions. With half the men, a quiet candidate has nowhere to hide — and no excuse not to lead.

  • Expect to be seen on every obstacle: plan, carry, and cross.
  • Speak your idea early. In a group of four, silence is read as having nothing to offer.
  • Physical effort counts double here — be the man who takes the heavy end.

  • Pick the topic you have a real incident for, not the one that sounds impressive.
  • Three points and a closing line. Say them in the first ten seconds in your head, then speak.
  • Start with the incident, not with a definition.
  • Stand still, speak to the whole group, finish before the time rather than tail off.

5 Physical Ability Test (PAT)

Each candidate attempts a set of physical challenges individually, evaluating physical fitness and determination. You are marked on how you attack the obstacle, not only on whether you clear it.

  • Typical individual obstacles: jumps, a balance beam, rope climb, a screen or wall, monkey crawl, a burma bridge and a double ditch.
  • Attempt every obstacle. A failed honest attempt scores; skipping does not.
  • Build the base now — running, pull-ups, rope work and core, six days a week.
  • Nurse no small injury quietly; report it and continue what you can.
